THE IDEA BEHIND SERPENTSHAPER

Nature is the starting point.
Curiosity does the rest.

An exploration of the relationship between venom, evolution, and the remarkable diversity of snakes.

WHAT IT DOES

What if venom
could take shape?

SerpentShaper is an interactive way to explore venom and imagine the snake behind it. Start with a real species or create a profile of your own, then adjust the proportions of broad toxin families to see an imagined snake take shape.

Switch between dorsal and ventral views and compare your inputs with reported venom profiles from real snakes. The tool also shares available reference information about those snakes, including habitat, prey, size, and published LD50 measurements.

THE INSPIRATION

Venom tells an
evolutionary story.

Snake venoms vary across species and even within the same species. Over evolutionary time, factors such as diet, prey, ecology, and ancestry can help shape the mixture of toxins a snake produces. Venom can play roles in capturing prey and defense, and its composition reflects a complex history rather than a single purpose.

SerpentShaper began with a question inspired by that diversity: if we change a venom profile, what kind of snake might we imagine around it? The project turns that question into a visual experiment grounded in real venom data.

LIMITATIONS

An exploration,
not a prediction.

Venom composition alone cannot determine what a snake looks like or reliably predict its habitat, diet, size, or toxicity. A generated snake is an informed illustration—not a real species, a species identification, or a biological or medical prediction. Reference traits and LD50 measurements describe real snakes, not the imagined animal.

The experimental model learns shape and markings from authored snake illustrations paired with venom profiles, rather than directly learning anatomy from photographs. A small photo-based color cue contributes to the color model. Source data can be incomplete and varies between samples, so the results should be treated as possibilities to explore, not established biological relationships.
