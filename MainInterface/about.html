Venom shaped
by evolution

Venom varies between snakes, even within the same species. Over evolutionary time, prey, ecology, and ancestry can influence its composition. Venom can help capture prey and defend against threats; its mixture reflects a complex history rather than a single purpose.

An illustration,
not a prediction

Venom alone cannot determine a snake’s appearance or reliably predict its habitat, diet, size, or toxicity. A generated snake is an exploratory illustration, not a species identification or a biological or medical prediction. Reference traits and LD50 measurements describe real snakes, not the imagined animal.

The experimental model learns shape and markings from authored illustrations paired with venom profiles, not anatomy directly from photographs. A small photo-based cue contributes to the color model. Sources can be incomplete and vary between samples, so results are possibilities to explore, not established biological relationships.
