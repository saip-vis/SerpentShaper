# Black mamba venom: follow-up source review

The 2025 primary study by [Redureau et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins17050243) examined pooled *Dendroaspis polylepis* venom from multiple Tanzanian snakes kept in captivity. Its trypsin-only, label-free LC-MS quantification reports 33.48% three-finger toxins, 55.62% venom Kunitz-type proteins, 1.02% metalloproteinases, 0.20% hyaluronidase, 0.04% natriuretic peptides and 9.64% in an aggregate other group for that sample, as read from the outer labels of figure 4D. These are relative peak-area-based estimates; see section 2.4 and figure 4D. A separate [partial machine-readable candidate](black_mamba_2025.json) records the values and their sampling context. The figure-level groups sum to 100%, but this is not a full molecular breakdown: the aggregate group is not identified at finer resolution, and categories not shown remain unknown. It is not part of the database, the interface or a training set.

The older workbook's black mamba row has 31.0% 3FTx, 61.1% Kunitz and 2.9% under NP. A [2017 review](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins9090290), table 1, reports these same three numbers, defines NP as natriuretic peptide, and cites [Laustsen et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jprot.2015.02.002). The cited 2015 paper labels NP as non-protein components and shows the same 2.9% in figure 2 (PDF page 50; legend on page 34). The source workbook has no row-level citation linking that particular row conclusively to the paper. This establishes a concrete labeling conflict between the 2017 review and the paper it cites, with strong numerical evidence the workbook may have inherited it; the workbook row's direct provenance remains unproven. A previous note incorrectly stated the 2015 value was 2.5%; inspecting the actual figure corrected that mistake.

A [later Ainsworth et al. study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jprot.2017.08.016), available online in 2017 and published in 2018, reanalyzed mass-spectrometry data from **Petras et al. 2016** with an expanded mamba toxin database and identified some low-mass peptides as natriuretic peptides. The reanalysis is of the Petras data, not demonstrated to be a reclassification of the 2015 Laustsen sample or the 2.9% workbook value. It establishes supporting evidence that some black mamba samples contain these peptides; it does not resolve this particular numeric entry.

The 2025 result and older candidate are from different study samples with different sources and methods: Tanzania versus a reported Kenyan pooled sample in the candidate paper. Their differences do not by themselves establish that either study was wrong or that one value should overwrite the other. The 2025 paper is the newest primary source found in this focused search, not proof that no newer publication exists.

**Decision:** retain the original workbook for provenance, exclude NP in profile `vd2023_e2d1aa974518_r189` from the main interface's loaded values and comparisons, the exploratory baseline and the length experiment, and preserve the 2025 results as a separate candidate. Before integrating the candidate, inspect its underlying quantitative data and define mappings, sampling provenance and compatibility with the existing database. The database and generated component metadata retain the original value, but it is not used as a reference measurement.

The publisher returned HTTP 429 when the supplementary-material link was opened on 22 November 2026. The complete underlying quantitative table has not been checked. The displayed figure groups have been transcribed, but the candidate remains excluded from ingestion.
