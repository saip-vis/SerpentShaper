# Image trait pilot

This is a small starting point for connecting photographs to visible traits: 15 photographs from 15 iNaturalist observations, grouped under five provider species labels. Each entry in `pilot_observations.json` records the source, attribution, image hash, existing review status, and provisional traits visible in that particular photograph. The five labels are spectacled cobra (*Naja naja*), coastal taipan (*Oxyuranus scutellatus*), black mamba (*Dendroaspis polylepis*), western diamondback (*Crotalus atrox*), and boomslang (*Dispholidus typus*).

These annotations were made by an assistant looking at the images, mostly at thumbnail scale. They are not expert-verified descriptions of the species. For example, a green boomslang photograph and a brown boomslang photograph are recorded separately; neither is assumed to describe every individual. Colors are approximate and `body_color` includes visible colors on a close-up head when no body is visible. A `null` means not visible, uncertain, or not assessed, never that the trait is absent. A raised snake without an annotated hood should not be interpreted as lacking a hood.

The original image review status and exclusion flags are retained. All pilot records are marked `pilot_training_eligible: false`: annotation here does not approve a photo, verify its species or licensing, screen it for injury, or override any exclusion. Some license versions are missing; consult the original source before reuse. Photos are not distributed in this folder, but each entry points to the local pilot and source page. Photographs and venom measurements are not known to come from the same individual, so joining by species would create a species-level association, not an individual-level measurement.

Run `python3 ML/image_traits/validate.py` from the repository root to check that the records still match their manifests, local files, hashes, and allowed trait values. The photographs are not included in Git, so this check requires the original downloaded image files to be present locally. This checks provenance and structure, not whether the annotations are biologically correct. No image model was trained and these records are not wired into the generator.

## Renderer cue and focused pattern experiment

`color_samples.json` defines two manually selected body patches on accepted western diamondback images from different observations. `python3 scripts/build_photo_cues.py` checks the manifest status, exclusion flags and hashes, then writes `rendering_cues.json` and the browser bundle. It extracts low- and high-luminance pixel colors from those patches. The renderer applies these weakly to compatible diamond markings; it does not train on them. The photo colors and the boxes are approximate, and the metadata status is not independent identification or a guarantee of condition. The individual photos are not included in Git, so regenerating this cue requires the original locally downloaded files, NumPy and Pillow. The checked-in browser bundle runs without them.

The `pattern_holdout.json` annotations are a separate three-photo check: whether large diamond-like markings are visible on each pictured portion. An unmarked visible portion does not prove the full animal lacks them. `python3 scripts/run_pattern_experiment.py` fits logistic regression from venom categories to manually authored preset pattern labels, compares it with an equal-species prevalence baseline using genus-held-out folds, and holds all three photographed genera out for the separate image check. It writes `pattern_experiment.json`; it requires the original local files, NumPy and scikit-learn. **No photos or photo annotations are used for training**, and the experimental model is not wired into the app. Each evaluation score describes a different small, imperfect task, not accuracy at generating snakes. Photo labels are not expert verified, and photographed animals are not linked to the venom samples.

The recorded genus-held-out run includes 148 species labels, only 11 with authored diamond patterns. At the default 0.5 cutoff, the unbalanced model identified none of those 11 positives; the prevalence baseline identified none either. A class-balanced variant identified 2 of 11 positives but made more false positive errors. On the separate three-photo check, both variants missed the one image with visible diamonds. Three photos do not establish generalization; the result is enough to leave this particular experimental classifier out of the renderer and try a better-defined task with more varied evidence.

One previously accepted photo (`inat_791396`) shows a suspicious area that could indicate injury; it was not used in the new cue or image check. This is a flag for further review, not a diagnosis, and the original manifest decision remains unchanged.

Next, inspect or correct the provisional labels and establish image/annotation and license review before considering them as training data. Preserve observation groups when splitting data so images of the same observation cannot appear in both training and evaluation sets.
